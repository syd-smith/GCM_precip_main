# Expected Future Precipitation Changes from the North American Monsoon

## Introduction
The Great Salt Lake Basin Integrated Plan (GSLBIP) is used to determine water allocation across the Salt Lake Region. To bild a plan for the future, a greater understanding of water entering the system through precipitation must be met through analysis of climatological variables. Global climate models (GCMs) are comprised of assumptions based on an initial condition and indication of change in future greenhouse gas emissions. They return predicted futurre variables, such as precipitation. Variables that might contribute to a change in monsoon patterns were also downloaded as NetCDF files. Variables for our initial anlysis included precipitation, northward near surface wind, eastward near surface wind, geopotential height, sea level pressure, and near surface air temperature (see era5_download.py for more information on the download process). Change in mean summer precipitation from the historical (1979-2014) to the future (2070-2099) period were graphed for each GCM. However, different models varied greatly in expected change of precipitation values through the end of the century. A large range in expected outcomes is likely due to the way a particular GCM simulates changes in the North American Monsoon (NAM). Clear and logical patterns appeared across the wet models that explain a future increase in precipitation.
